1. Installing and starting

Millennium is built from its source code. You need Rust (stable, 1.88 or newer) and Git. It runs on macOS, Windows and Linux.

git clone https://github.com/rsantacroce/millennium.git
cd millennium
cargo run --release -p client

The first build takes a few minutes (Bevy is big). After that, starting is quick.

To play you need a world to join. Either:

  • Host one yourself: click Host Game on the sign-on window. Your computer runs the world and your friends join it (see Hosting).
  • Join a friend's or a dedicated server: type its address (for example 192.168.1.20:5888) in the Server field.

2. Accounts and characters

  1. Type a screen name and a password (at least 8 characters) and click New Account. Next time, use Sign On.
  2. Create a character: pick a body type, skin tone, jacket, pants and cap. Each account holds up to three characters.
  3. Click Play. You arrive at Maple & 5th, Downtown, with $25 in your pocket.

The first time you connect to a server, the game remembers its certificate (like SSH does). If that server is ever reinstalled you'll get a warning; see Troubleshooting.

Passwords are hashed with argon2id and travel over TLS: the server never sees or stores them in plain text.

4. The screen

  • Top left: your name, the zone, your cash, the time of day and the main keys. Under it, your health, and any food buffs.
  • Top right: the minimap (N). People show as dots, you as yellow.
  • Bottom left: the Millennium Messenger: chat tabs, system notices, and the line to type in.
  • Bottom right: the quest tracker. It shows the next step of up to three quests, and Tips with your own key bindings.
  • Over people's heads: a yellow ! means they have a job for you; a ? means you have something to hand in.
  • Green glowing patches are exits to other zones. Their names show when you're close.
  • Tooltips: rest the mouse on an item (in your bag, a shop, e-Bid, a trade or your footlocker), a skill, a reputation line or a button, and a yellow box explains it.
  • The map (M) shows the whole zone from above: streets, buildings with their names, exits, people and you.

Buildings and trees between the camera and you turn see-through, so you never lose your character.

5. Your first day

Welcome to the City

You arrive next to Officer Pruitt, outside the Byte Cafe. Walk up and press E. He'd like a Fizz Cola from Mr. Kim's Corner Store, across Maple Street. Talk to Mr. Kim, choose to shop, click Buy, and bring the soda back. Pruitt pays you, gives you a first aid kit and tells you where the work is.

56k Dreams (your first repair)

Dot, outside the Byte Cafe, needs a modem. Sal at Pawn & Pager has a fried one, and Mr. Kim sells the Screwdriver Set you need. Open your inventory (I), click the modem and press Use. Stand still while you repair it: moving cancels. Bring it back to Dot for cash, Electronics XP and a pager. Dot also opens the e-Bid terminal for you.

Rat Problem (your first fight)

Take the Metro (the green doorway at the south-east corner of Downtown) to the Harbor Docks. Foreman Rizzo wants five Dock Rats knocked out. Buy a baseball bat from Sal first ($30) and a couple of first aid kits from Mr. Kim. See Fighting.

After that it's up to you. Every quest in the game, who gives it and what it pays is listed on the asset list.

8. Making money

  • Repairs: buy broken gadgets cheap (Sal's pawn shop has a few at a time), repair them and sell them on. All recipes are on the asset list.
  • Selling: select an item in your inventory while a shop is open and press Sell $N. Each shop only buys the kinds of things it deals in (the pawn shop takes anything, for the least).
  • Supply and demand: every sale of the same item lowers that shop's price by 8% for a while (down to 35%); it recovers over time. Spread your sales around.
  • Limited stock: cheap repair inputs sell out and restock over time. The shop shows how many are left.
  • Quests pay cash, XP, items and reputation. Hacks pay small amounts of cash. Rivals drop cash and loot.

Your bag holds 20 stacks. Shops never pay less than $1, and quest items can't be sold or traded.

9. e-Bid, the auction house

Talk to Dot (after 56k Dreams) or Wendy at the mall's Gadget Shack and ask for the terminal.

  • Browse: search, then Bid (always the minimum bid) or Buy at the buyout price.
  • Sell: lists the item selected in your inventory, with a starting bid, an optional buyout and a duration. The item leaves your bag until it sells or the auction ends.
  • My auctions: cancel ones nobody has bid on yet.
  • Claims: collect everything waiting for you: things you won, money from sales, and refunds when someone outbids you. It works even if the other player is offline.

Listing costs 5% of the starting price; e-Bid keeps 5% of a sale. Your bid money is held until you're outbid or you win.

10. Fighting

  • Click a rival, or press Tab to target the nearest one (again for the next).
  • R starts and stops auto-attacking; you swing on your weapon's timer. F is a power swing (once every 8 seconds).
  • Damage comes from your weapon (fists, a baseball bat, a tire iron...) plus your Fitness. Armor from clothing takes some off every hit you take.
  • Use a First Aid Kit (or eat) to heal mid-fight.
  • Some rivals attack on sight; most only fight back. They give up the chase if you run far enough.
  • Health comes back slowly a few seconds after the last hit. Getting knocked out just wakes you up at the zone's safe spot (by the Metro downtown), with full health and nothing lost.
  • In a party, members within 30 m share the XP and quest credit.

The Galleria Mall is a no-fighting zone. Drivers can't fight, and rivals ignore them. Who is where and how tough they are: Rivals.

11. Clothing, food and buffs

  • Clothing: caps, jackets, shoes and accessories (shades, a pager, a walkman...). Use one to wear it; the inventory lists what you're wearing, with Off buttons. Clothing gives armor, extra health and bonus skill levels, and shows on your character.
  • Food: heals, and some meals give a temporary bonus in a skill (shown on the HUD). Eating again refreshes the buff; buffs don't stack and end when you log out.

12. Factions and reputation

Four factions run the city: City Services, Meridian Corp, the Dockside Crew and the Zer0 Day hackers. Quests, fights and hacks raise or lower your standing with them, from Hostile through Unfriendly, Neutral, Friendly and Honored to Revered.

Faction shops give 5% off when you're Friendly, 10% when Honored and 15% when Revered, and keep some of their best gear for good customers. Some dialogue only opens up with enough reputation. Watch out: hacking Meridian's ATM won't make you friends there.

13. Hacking

  1. Find something hackable (they have a green name tag): the payphone by the Byte Cafe, the arcade's high-score table, Meridian's ATM, the factory's security panel, the pirate radio antenna, the harbor office terminal...
  2. Walk up, press E and choose Hack it.
  3. A green terminal flashes an access code (0-9, A-F). Memorize it before it turns into ????.
  4. Type it and press Enter before the timer runs out (Esc gives up).

Success pays cash and Hacking XP, sometimes loot, and changes reputation. That hack then cools down for you for a while. Failure trips an alarm: City Services reputation drops and you wait 30 seconds.

Every 3 levels you have over a hack's requirement removes a digit (never fewer than 3), and the code stays on screen longer. Gear helps: the Acoustic Coupler, the PalmPilot and the ThinkBook laptop. Glitch, behind the Byte Cafe, starts you off with Phreaking 101. All hacks and their payouts: Hacks.

14. Cars, the radio and races

  • Getting a car: Old Earl at the Abandoned Factory will give you his old hatchback if you get it running (the quest Earl's Junker: a rebuilt carburetor, a car battery, two spark plugs and Mechanics level 3).
  • Driving: G gets in when "[G] Drive your ..." shows. Steer with WASD, or click where you want to go. Pull back while stopped to reverse and turn around. G again (when stopped) gets out. Cars stay parked where you leave them, even when you log off.
  • The radio: T cycles Z-100 Hits (Eurodance), Smooth 98.7 (bossa) and Radio Free Maple (jungle, from the pirate transmitter on the rooftops), or off.
  • Races: Dash runs the Maple & 5th Sprint downtown, and Trucker Mo a highway race from the Gas-N-Go on Route 9. Hit every checkpoint before the clock runs out. See Street races.

15. Houses

  • Five houses in Maple Heights are for sale ($400). Talk to a front door to buy one, go in, or ring the bell (which lets you in if you're in the owner's party).
  • Furniture comes from Home Comforts. At home, Use it from your inventory to put it down; next to a piece, E uses it and P picks it up.
  • Home perks: a stove counts as a frying pan, a workbench helps Mechanics, a computer lets you practice hacking, a waterbed leaves you Well Rested, a TV has something on.
  • The footlocker stores 20 stacks: open it and select an item in your inventory to store it.

16. Day and night

A day lasts 40 real minutes (server owners can change this). At night the streetlamps and windows come on, the music changes, Vinnie's hot dog cart opens downtown, and graffiti taggers come out. The time is on the HUD.

17. Chat, trading and friends

Press Enter to chat. Plain text goes to the current tab's channel. Commands:

/w name msg (/whisper, /t, /tell), /r msgPrivate message to a character; reply to the last one.
/p /party, /c /crewYour party / your crew.
/trade nameAsk someone nearby (8 m) to trade.
/invite name, /leave, /kick nameParties (up to 5).
/friend add name, /friend remove nameYour friends list (you get a notice when they come online).
/crew create Name TAG, /crew invite, /crew kick, /crew leave, /crew disbandCrews: guilds with a tag on your name.
/ignore name, /unignore name, /ignorelistStop seeing someone's messages.

Trades: both players see both offers. Add items with Offer in your inventory and cash with the buttons. Any change un-accepts both sides, so nobody can swap something out at the last second. It goes through when you both accept.

Messages are limited to 255 characters, and spamming earns a short automatic mute.

18. Settings

F10 opens the settings: sound effects and music volume, interface size, shadows, street lights at night, camera speed, and every key binding (click one, then press the new key; a key that's already used swaps over). They're saved in saves/settings.toml.

19. Hosting and running a server

Host Game

Click Host Game on the sign-on window (after typing a name and password). Your computer runs the world and signs you in; the HUD shows the address your friends type in the Server field. The world is saved to saves/world.db.

  • Friends on the same network can join straight away.
  • Over the internet, forward TCP and UDP port 5888 on your router, or use a virtual LAN such as Tailscale or ZeroTier.
  • You're an admin of your own world (/mute, /unmute).

A dedicated server

docker compose up -d          # PostgreSQL on port 5433
cargo run --release -p server

No Docker? Set url = "sqlite://saves/server.db" under [database] in config/server.toml. The same file sets the ports, the length of a day, the chat rules and the admins. Large worlds can run each zone on its own server process, linked by a hub; see the README.

20. Troubleshooting

  • "The server's identity has changed": the server was reinstalled. If you trust it, remove its line from saves/known_servers.txt.
  • "There's no account named ...": click New Account first.
  • "Logged in, but the game server didn't answer": the login (TCP) got through but the game (UDP) didn't. Check the firewall or port forwarding for UDP 5888.
  • "Could not listen on port 5888": another server or hosted game is already running.
  • Stuck walking somewhere? Press any movement key: WASD always wins.
  • "You need to get closer": walk right up to the person, or just click them.
  • Start over: delete the saves/ folder (hosted worlds, certificates, settings).

Something missing or wrong? Open an issue.
